NEARLY TWO CENTURIES OF ADVENTURE

Founded in the pristine Blackwood Valley, our camp has empowered young leaders with resilience, teamwork, and independence since the 1830s.

1838 – 1870

The Frontier Outpost

Established in 1838 by pioneer woodcutter Ebenezer "Timber" Sterling, the property began as a river trade post providing shelter, land navigation advice, and warm stew to traveling settlers.

1884 – 1920

The Youth Wilderness Guild

In 1884, Eleanor Sterling converted the outpost into a retreat for young industrial workers, teaching physical vigor, trail building, blacksmithing, and campfire ethics.

1934 – 1950

Civilian Conservation Corps (CCC) Era

Designated as CCC Site 412 in 1934, workers quarried local riverstone to build our famous Grand Dining Lodge, hand-carved cedar cabins, and waterfront docks.

1976 – 1999

Environmental Leadership Sanctuary

Transitioning into a formal youth foundation in 1976, the camp focused on ecological conservation, high-adventure survival, kayaking, and self-reliance.

2000s – Present

The Modern Transition Camp

Recognizing the jump to high school as a crucial life milestone, we revamped our curriculum to combine high ropes and wilderness skill-building with esports strategy, culinary challenges, and high school readiness leadership.
